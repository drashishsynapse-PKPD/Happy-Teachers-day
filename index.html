Still learning from
respected faculty

Everything I know about clinical pharmacology, teachers taught me.

A few of the things they taught me, and thanks for each one.

the protocol

The protocol
writing sessions

For the rationale as well as the format — randomisation, blinding, endpoints, sample size, the analysis plan, and the reason behind each one.

A promise made to patients not yet met. Thank you to the department for teaching me to treat it as one.

consent

How to take it
properly

In the language the patient thinks in. The purpose, the risks and the alternatives said plainly, and the right to refuse stated out loud — treatment unchanged either way.

Then the signature, and the date.

Taught patiently, and never hurried. Thank you.

reactions

the teaching

How it was actually
handed over

In a calendar, kept week after week by teachers who made room for it.

  • Journal club — where I learnt to read the methods before the conclusions
  • Seminars — topics chosen carefully for me, never handed out at random
  • PG discussions — questions welcomed, and answered until they were truly settled
  • UG discussions
  • Experimental pharmacology

Thank you for every hour of it.

Thank you

For the reasoning behind every line of a protocol. For the discussions that ran late.

Without the guidance of my respected faculty I would have stayed a partial agonist — bound to the work, producing a fraction of what it deserved. With their blessing, I hope to become a full agonist of clinical pharmacology.

Happy Teachers' Day.

Sincerely grateful, Ashish
